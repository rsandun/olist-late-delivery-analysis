# Executive Summary: Late Deliveries at Olist

## Bottom line
Only 8% of orders arrive late, but those orders create most of our unhappy customers. About 5,000 orders that are more than 3 days late hold 90% of the revenue at risk (574,359 of 639,252). A focused fix is worth doing.

## What we found
1. **Lateness hurts reviews.** A customer with a late order is about 6 times more likely to leave a bad review (54% vs 9%). This holds in all 26 product categories we checked.
2. **The damage starts after 3 days.** Review score is 4.29 on time, 3.77 at 1-3 days late, 2.32 at 4-7 days late, and 1.73 at 8+ days late.
3. **The problem is regional.** Alagoas has almost 1 in 4 orders late. The five north-east states (AL, MA, PI, CE, SE) average 17.4% late, against 7.8% elsewhere.
4. **The cause is a slow group of orders, not the average.** Promised dates are already longer in the north-east, so a longer promise is not the answer.

## What we recommend
**Operations**
- Start with the five north-east states.
- Track orders still in transit after their expected date, and investigate the slowest 10% with carriers and sellers.
- Put the top sellers by revenue at risk on a watch list.
- Target: no order more than 3 days late.

**Marketing**
- Send an apology and a small voucher to customers whose order will be more than 3 days late.
- Test first on half of those orders and compare reviews and repeat purchases after 1 to 2 months.

## What it is worth (estimate)
The voucher plan costs about 80,000 and breaks even if it recovers 14% of at-risk revenue.

| Case | Recovered | Net gain |
|---|---|---|
| Worst | 10% | -22,520 |
| Normal | 25% | +63,634 |
| Best | 40% | +149,788 |

## What to keep in mind
Revenue at risk is not proven lost money. Recovery rates are assumptions, so the test comes before a full launch. The data is from 2016 to 2018. We do not yet know why the north-east is slow.
